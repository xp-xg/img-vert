Quick answer: AVIF vs WebP

  • Use WebP if you want broad compatibility and simple implementation — it's the safe, practical modern choice for 2026.
  • Use AVIF with a WebP fallback if you're optimising aggressively and can implement the <picture> element for fallbacks.
  • Don't use JPG or PNG for new web projects — both WebP and AVIF are meaningfully better for web use.

What is AVIF?

AVIF (AV1 Image File Format) is a modern image format based on the AV1 video codec, developed by the Alliance for Open Media — a consortium including Google, Mozilla, Apple, Netflix, and Amazon.

Like WebP, AVIF supports both lossy and lossless compression, transparency, and animation. Its key advantage over WebP is better compression efficiency — it can achieve smaller files at the same visual quality. Its key disadvantages are slower encoding times and less complete browser support.

AVIF is to WebP what WebP was to JPEG: a generational improvement in compression efficiency, with the trade-offs of a newer format (adoption lag, tooling immaturity).

WebP's 3–5% advantage in browser coverage translates to millions of users. More importantly, AVIF's unsupported browsers include older Safari versions still in active use among Apple users — a significant demographic for many sites.

Encoding speed

This is AVIF's biggest practical weakness. AVIF encoding is significantly slower than WebP — often 5–20x slower depending on compression level and image size.

  • A 2MB JPEG converts to WebP in under a second on most hardware
  • The same image to AVIF can take 5–30 seconds at high quality settings

For individual images this barely matters. For batch processing hundreds of product images or server-side on-the-fly conversion, the difference is significant. Many CDN and image optimisation services charge more for AVIF conversion or don't support it yet.

Quality comparison

At very low quality settings (high compression), AVIF clearly wins — WebP develops blocky artefacts while AVIF maintains more natural-looking degradation. At medium to high quality settings (where most web images should be), the difference is often imperceptible without side-by-side comparison at 100% zoom.

AVIF has a particular strength with smooth gradients, skies, skin tones, and areas of uniform colour. WebP is more consistent across different content types.

When to use AVIF vs WebP

Use WebP when:

  • You want simple implementation with no fallback complexity
  • Your audience includes older Safari users (pre-Safari 16)
  • You're batch-processing many images and encoding speed matters
  • Your CDN or image service has great WebP support but limited AVIF support
  • You want the safest, most universally compatible modern format

Use AVIF (with WebP fallback) when:

  • File size minimisation is a top priority (high-traffic sites, mobile-first)
  • You have a modern build pipeline that can generate multiple formats
  • Your audience is predominantly on Chrome or modern Safari
  • You're a developer comfortable with the <picture> element

How to serve both AVIF and WebP with a JPG fallback

The HTML <picture> element lets you offer multiple formats — the browser picks the first one it supports:

<picture>
  <source srcset="image.avif" type="image/avif">
  <source srcset="image.webp" type="image/webp">
  <img src="image.jpg" alt="Description" loading="lazy">
</picture>

This approach delivers AVIF to browsers that support it, WebP to the next tier, and JPG as the universal fallback — giving every user the most efficient format their browser can handle.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is AVIF better than WebP?

AVIF produces smaller files — often 20–50% smaller than WebP at equivalent quality. However, WebP has broader browser support, faster encoding, and simpler tooling. For most projects in 2026, WebP is the practical choice.

What browsers support AVIF?

Chrome, Firefox, Safari 16+, and Edge — approximately 91–93% of browsers globally in 2026. Safari 14–15 supports WebP but not AVIF.

Should I switch from WebP to AVIF?

Not necessarily. Consider using AVIF with a WebP fallback using the <picture> element. This gives AVIF to supported browsers and WebP to everyone else, without breaking compatibility.

Does Google PageSpeed Insights recommend AVIF?

Google's PageSpeed Insights and Lighthouse flag "Serve images in next-gen formats" — they recommend both WebP and AVIF. Either satisfies the recommendation.
